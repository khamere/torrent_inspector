What you get, in one minute

Install it, open a listing, and three things are different.

1 · Every title is judged

A ✓, ✕ or ? beside each release name. Hover for the reason in one line; click for the full explanation, the corrections and a copyable report.

2 · The group is a link

The -GROUP at the end of a name opens where that group is internal, its other releases here, and the same search on your trackers.

3 · The MediaInfo is checked

On a torrent's page, what the title claims is compared with the report the uploader posted — resolution, codecs, audio, HDR, languages.

Every loaded title, checked as the page renders

Listings, a member's uploads, bookmarks, playlists, the home page panels, a film's own page, and the moderation queues.

✓ The Money Pit 1986 1080p BluRay DTS 5.1 x264-iJiA

Passed. The supported title checks found nothing wrong — not a promise that the media, the source history or tracker approval were verified.

✕ Dead.Silence.2007.2160p.UHD.BluRay.DTS-HD.MA.5.1.DV.HDR.x265-B0MBARDiERS

Corrections found. The guide writes display titles with spaces, so it writes the fix out for you — Dead Silence 2007 2160p UHD BluRay DTS-HD MA 5.1 DV HDR x265-B0MBARDiERS — keeping the dots that belong, like H.264 and DDP5.1.

? Bottle Top Bill 2005 540p WEB-DL AAC 2.0 H.264

Your judgement needed. An uncertain category, a rule the title alone cannot settle, or a missing group tag. It asks instead of guessing.

Whose rules?

A Rules dropdown in the bar: DarkPeers, Zenith, or a tracker you add. It changes the upload rules and the banned-group list — the naming guide itself is shared — so the same title can be clean on one tracker and rejected on another.

Two rule sets are already written

LUME and OnlyEncodes+ ship with the script, built from their own published guides — 14 and 16 rules, and OnlyEncodes' 136 banned groups with the reasons and dates its page gives. Press Add once and they join the Rules list.

Nothing is added until you press it: adding a rule set changes which rules a badge cites, and that is your call.

Add your own tracker

Tracker rules… takes a tracker's banned list pasted straight off its page, plus tick-boxes for the rules that apply. Profiles export and import as JSON.

Build one on the web → No install needed, which is the point: a tracker's staff can write its official profile, check it and try a release name against it before handing it to anyone. That page runs these same modules, so what it says is what the script says.

Audit a whole sweep

Audit loaded titles lists every name on the page with its faults as copyable text, and remembers what you checked while browsing so one audit can cover many pages.

Release groups, and where they are internal

Click the group tag at the end of any release name.

What the menu shows

Listed as internal at … — every tracker, not just one. DON comes back HDBits and UHDBits; LoRD comes back Bit-HDTV, HD-Torrents and UHDBits.

Then that group's other releases on the tracker you are on, and the same search on each tracker you have chosen for the cross-check.

Where the list comes from

It ships with a community directory of internal groups — 853 groups across 151 trackers — dated and cited in the source. Because a directory can go stale, every line reads “listed as internal at”, and a group that is not on it is reported as not on it rather than guessed at.

What you add sits on top of that directory rather than replacing it, so a group added to the shipped list in a later version still reaches you.

Why some are not links

A tracker's line links to it when the script already has that tracker's address, from your cross-check list. Private tracker domains move, so it will not invent one — those lines stay as plain text until you supply an address.

Adding a group takes about five seconds

  1. From the release itself. Click the group tag. If it has no home yet, the menu says so and offers “Add GRiMM to a tracker” — type the tracker (your known ones are suggested), press Add, and the menu redraws with the new entry. Nothing else to open.
  2. From the list. In a listing bar, Internal groups… sits next to Tracker rules…. It has an Add row — group, tracker, Add — which appends to the list.
  3. In bulk. The same panel holds the whole list as text, one tracker per line. Paste a directory page in as it stands, or write lines yourself: 
    OnlyEncodes+|OnlyWeb GRiMM
BeyondHD|BHDStudio FraMeSToR FLUX|https://beyond-hd.me
    A third field gives that whole tracker an address, so its lines become links.

The two Add paths append; only “Save list” replaces the whole list. Save tells you what it did — “Saved: 434 entries”, or “— 3 fewer than before” — so a mis-paste announces itself. Clear list puts the shipped directory back.

If another script has already turned that group tag into a link, this one leaves it completely alone — no wrapping, no rewriting, nothing for two scripts to fight over.

Moderating a queue

On /torrents/pending, /torrents/rejected and /torrents/postponed.

A decision log

Each row gets Approved / Rejected / Asked the uploader / Left for someone else, ticked with the date so a sweep does not repeat itself. Rejecting or asking prompts for a note.

It records what you decided. It changes nothing on the tracker and never leaves your browser.

A reply, written from the findings

Inside the badge dialog: a draft with the corrections numbered in the guide's own words, the release name and its link, and a closing line. Four outcomes, one suggested — a banned group suggests a rejection, not a rename.

Manual checks appear as worth confirming, never as faults. Edit before you copy; nothing is sent.

What the page itself says

TMDB title and year against the name. The Type and Resolution filed against what the name claims. The file list — containers, samples, a lone film in a folder, whether a season pack's files agree. Whether MediaInfo or BDInfo was posted. And Dual-Audio / MULTi against the page's own original language.

Where a rule is in hand it is cited, and the finding is an error. Where none is, the finding says so — “the guide in hand states no container rule for this tracker, so check its upload rules.” Nothing invents a rule to sound authoritative.

The MediaInfo already on the page, read properly

Alt+Shift+I on a torrent page — or paste a report of your own.

The title claimsChecked against the report
1080p, 2160pThe actual pixel dimensions, and progressive against the scan type
x264, x265, AV1The video format the report names
DD+ 5.1, DTS-HD MA, AtmosThe default audio track — DD and DD+ kept apart — and the channel layout against the reported count
DV, HDR10+Whether that HDR format is actually there
Dual-Audio, MULTiThe languages in the file, and subtitles for non-English audio
REMUXChapters, and a TrueHD track's compatibility track where the tracker requires one

Compare two releases — the vs button

On a torrent page, press vs and capture this release; open the other one and capture that. You get the file lists and sizes compared (with the gap named), the two MediaInfo reports compared, the tags and encode settings each file carries, and both releases written out in the block layout WinMerge or meld reads. It does not pick a winner: source, encode quality and provenance are not in a report, and it says so.

Sizes are compared on the exact byte count the page carries, not the rounded figure it shows — so two torrents that both read 5.70 GiB and differ by a megabyte are reported as differing, which the rounded numbers hide.

Both captures stay in your browser. No companion program, no new permission.

Check a title before you upload

The naming box checks against the chosen tracker's rules, with reference fields — official title, original-language title, service — that make the manual checks decidable, and a searchable list of the 288 streaming-service abbreviations.

[ MULTIPLE FILES ] — a pack, in one click

Any torrent holding more than one file says so under its name. Click the marker and the whole list goes to your clipboard: the folder and its total, then every file with its exact size.

One file gets the same treatment with the thing you actually want off a one-file page — its size to the byte: [ SINGLE FILE · 2449415267 B ], and one click copies the name and that count. Where the page carries no exact figure the marker shows the page’s own rounded wording instead, rather than a number nobody printed.

Bureau.Burgwallen.S01.720p.WEB-DL.AAC.2.0.x264-DDF 9974836899 B
Bureau.Burgwallen.S01E01.…-DDF.mkv 1221811248 B
Bureau.Burgwallen.S01E02.…-DDF.mkv 1278590592 B

Nothing is copied until you press it.

The comment you type every day, once

Beside the comment box, next to Write · Preview, a ⤵ button fills your template into it. Write {title}, {size}, {bytes}, {files}, {count}, {url}, {id} or {date} and it fills them in from the page you are on — {files} writing the same listing the marker above copies.

Anything else in braces is left exactly as you typed it, so your BBCode is safe, and a token the page cannot answer is left blank and named back to you rather than posted as the word {bytes}.

It writes into the box and stops. It does not post, does not submit, and does not overwrite a draft you have started — you press post yourself.

Private notes

Per torrent, kept in your browser, never sent anywhere.

Is it already on a tracker you are on?

Links, built on the page and opened only when you click them.

  • Already uploaded? — the exact release name searched on every tracker you have chosen, so you find out before you spend the effort. Exact means exact: a different edition or group will not match, and it says so.
  • On a request — every row on /requests gets a search button, and a request's own page shows the links under its title. 60 trackers are built in — 43 of them UNIT3D — each with an editable search address; anything missing can be added.
  • Other versions — what else exists here, beside IMDb, TMDB, Trakt, Letterboxd, JustWatch, Blu-ray.com, srrDB, and MyAnimeList / MusicBrainz / Open Library / IGDB as the category warrants.
  • Ticked once, ticked everywhere. The trackers you are on follow you between every site the script runs on, rather than being an empty list again on each one. So do the trackers you have added, their rules, and your internal-groups list.
  • Search all opens them in tabs. Browsers allow one tab per click and silently block the rest, so if yours refuses a burst they are offered one at a time, with Copy every link as the fallback.

Only https addresses are accepted, a tracker that cannot carry the search is left out with the reason, and no login, cookie, passkey or API key is read or stored.

Installing it

  1. Install Tampermonkey if you do not have it.
  2. Click Install the script. Tampermonkey recognises a .user.js address and opens its own install page.
  3. Read what it asks for — the matches and permissions are listed at the top — then press Install.
  4. Reload the tracker. The bar appears above the listing.

Which trackers it runs on

43 UNIT3D trackers, DarkPeers and Zenith among them. Which sites run UNIT3D comes from HDVinnie's Private Trackers Spreadsheet; the addresses come from each tracker's own Jackett or Prowlarr indexer definition, because that spreadsheet carries none. A few UNIT3D sites have no definition to read and are left out rather than guessed at — they are named in the README, and adding one takes a line. Anything not on the list is one // @match away.

Every tracker is a // @match line at the top of the file: delete a line to stop it running there, copy one and change the domain to add your own. The list is generated from the tracker catalogue when the script is built, so the two cannot drift apart.

Writing a profile for a tracker of your own? The rules builder does it in a browser, with no install, and checks it before you paste it in.
Rules it has out of the box: DarkPeers and Zenith, built in, and LUME and OnlyEncodes+, one Add away and self-selecting on their own trackers. Anywhere else nothing is badged until you choose a rule set from the Rules picker in the bar, or add that tracker under Tracker rules…. A red ✕ on LST citing DarkPeers’ banned list would be quoting a rulebook LST never wrote, so it waits to be told. Everything that needs no rulebook — the Inspector, the vs comparison, the group tags, the cross-check — works there straight away.

Updates come to you. From 1.25.0 the script carries the address it is published at, so Tampermonkey checks on its own schedule and offers the new version. Prefer to do it by hand? Open the file on GitHub, copy it, and paste it over the contents of a new Tampermonkey script.

Upgrading from before 1.26.0? It was called DarkPeers - Torrent Inspector then. It is not DarkPeers’ script and never was — it runs on 43 trackers — so it is now just Torrent Inspector. Tampermonkey identifies an installed script by what is in its header, so the rename can arrive as a second entry in your script list rather than as an update to the first. If you see two, keep the new one and delete the old, or you get two of every badge. Your setup should come with you: everything the script saves is written both to Tampermonkey’s own store and to ordinary browser storage on the tracker you were using, and a fresh install picks the second one up. If you have a lot set up, press Export under Tracker rules… → Added trackers first.

If you download the file instead, do not double-click it — Windows tries to run a .js file rather than install it. Keep one copy enabled. If the full DKOKTO Scene Edition is running on the same page, this one stands aside so the two never clash.

Tutorials

Six things people actually do with it, start to finish. Every one of them is reading and clipboard only — nothing here writes to a tracker.

1 · Your first five minutes

From installed to badges on the page.

  1. Install and reload. On DarkPeers and Zenith the badges appear straight away — those two are built in. On LUME and OnlyEncodes+ they appear as soon as you add that rule set (step 3): each profile names its own tracker, so on that tracker it selects itself and you never touch the dropdown. Anywhere else nothing is judged yet — that is deliberate.
  2. Choose whose rules apply here. The listing bar has a Rules dropdown. Pick the tracker you are actually on if it is there; otherwise pick the rule set closest to it. The choice is remembered per tracker, so choosing DarkPeers on one site does not change what applies on another.
  3. Add a ready-made rule set if it fits. Tracker rules… → Added trackers lists LUME and OnlyEncodes+ under Rule sets that ship with this script. Press Add once and they join the Rules dropdown — and on luminarr.me or onlyencodes.cc that is the whole setup, because the profile carries that tracker's address and applies itself there.
  4. Tick the trackers you are on. Open Is this already on your trackers? and tick them. This list follows you to every tracker the script runs on, so you only do it once.
  5. Hover a badge. One line tells you why. Click it for the corrections and a copyable report.

Nothing is badged on a tracker whose rules it does not have. A red ✕ citing another tracker's banned list would be quoting a rulebook that tracker never wrote. Everything that needs no rulebook — the Inspector, vs, group tags, the cross-check — works there immediately.

2 · Sweeping a pending queue

For moderators, on /torrents/pending.

  1. Read the bar first. It counts the errors, passes and reviews on the page, so you know how much of the queue needs you before you start scrolling.
  2. Work the ✕ rows. Click the badge: the corrections are listed in the guide's own words, with the fixed title written out where the fault is mechanical.
  3. Use the draft reply. The badge dialog builds one from the findings — the release name, its link, the numbered corrections, a closing line. Four outcomes are offered and one is suggested; a banned group suggests a rejection rather than a rename. Edit it, then copy it. Nothing is sent.
  4. Tick what you decided. Each row takes Approved / Rejected / Asked the uploader / Left for someone else with the date, so the next sweep does not repeat itself. It records what you decided; it changes nothing on the tracker.
  5. Audit the sweep. Audit loaded titles gives every name with its faults as copyable text, and remembers pages you have already been through so one audit can cover several.

The amber ? rows are not failures — they are the checks a title alone cannot settle. They ask instead of guessing, and the reply builder shows them as worth confirming rather than as faults.

3 · Settling a trump with vs

Two releases of the same film, and a decision to justify.

  1. Open the first torrent and press vs in the lookup row, then Capture this page as A.
  2. Open the other — it can be on a different tracker, as long as the same script runs there — and capture it as B.
  3. Read the three tables. The files and sizes (with the exact byte counts and the gap between them), the two MediaInfo reports field by field, and the tags and encode settings each container carries.
  4. Copy what you need. Copy the differences gives the findings in words for a report; Copy both blocks gives both releases in the layout WinMerge or meld reads.

It will not pick a winner. Source, encode quality and provenance are not in a file list or a MediaInfo report. Where both torrents carry the same release name and the files differ, it does conclude one thing — a release is one file, so at least one of them is not the release its name claims — and it says plainly that neither page can tell you which.

4 · Teaching it a tracker's rules

When you are on a tracker it does not know yet.

  1. Try a ready-made one first. Tracker rules… → Added trackers: LUME and OnlyEncodes+ are already written from their published guides. Adding one is the same as pasting its JSON, and you can edit it afterwards.
  2. Or build one from the form. The Build one tab takes the tracker's name and address, its banned list pasted straight off the page in whatever shape it publishes — a table, pipes, runs of spaces, one per line — and tick-boxes for the common rules.
  3. Check what you are about to add. Show the JSON prints it before anything is saved. A pattern that will not compile is refused with the reason rather than swallowed.
  4. Keep it or share it. Copy JSON and Save as file export a profile; the Paste or load tab takes one back. Profiles follow you between trackers like the rest of your setup.

A profile is data, not code: patterns are compiled as regular expressions and matched against a title, and nothing in one is ever run.

5 · A season pack, start to finish

The list, the sizes, and whether the pack agrees with itself.

  1. Open the torrent. If it holds more than one file, [ MULTIPLE FILES ] sits under the release name.
  2. Click it. The folder and its total, then every file with its exact byte count, land on your clipboard — ready to paste into a report or a helpdesk ticket without retyping anything.
  3. Read the page findings. Under the name: whether the files in the pack agree with each other and with the title, what containers they use, whether there is a stray file or a sample, and whether MediaInfo or BDInfo was posted at all.
  4. Check the numbering. Season packs, multi-season packs, daily-show dates, specials and extras each have their own form, and the finding names the one the chosen tracker asks for.

6 · Checking a title before you upload

Catch it in the box rather than in a postponed queue.

  1. Open the naming box — Alt+Shift+I on a torrent page, or the toolbar — and paste the title you intend to use.
  2. Pick the category and the tracker. The rules that apply come from the rule set chosen for that tracker, so the same title can be clean on one and rejected on another.
  3. Fill the reference fields. The official title, the original-language title and the service turn several “cannot be decided from the title” reviews into real answers.
  4. Search the service list if you are unsure of an abbreviation — 288 of them, searchable, with the spelling each tracker's guide expects.
  5. Paste your MediaInfo. The Inspector compares what the report says with what the title claims: resolution, codecs, the default audio track and its channels, HDR, languages.

Already using Upload Assistant? Nothing here touches the upload form. It checks the name and the report, and leaves the uploading to the tool you already use.

7 · The comment you leave on every upload

Write it once; fill it in from the page after that.

  1. Open Templates…, beside the comment box on any torrent page. Two templates: one for a comment, one for a description, each with a tick to turn it on.
  2. Write yours in the Comment box. Plain text and BBCode, plus any of the tokens — {title}, {size}, {bytes}, {files}, {count}, {url}, {id}, {date}. The editor tells you which ones it found as you type.
  3. Save, and close it. It is kept in the script's own storage, so it is the same template on every tracker the script runs on, not one per site.
  4. Press ⤵ beside the comment box. The template goes in with the tokens filled from the page you are on. Anything a page cannot answer is left blank, and the button tells you which.
  5. Read it, edit it, post it. The button never posts — it fills the box and stops.

Doing release notes rather than a comment? The description template has no insert button, because descriptions are written on the upload form and this script stays off it. Copy it from the editor instead — the tokens are filled the same way.

Questions people actually ask

Sixty trackers — how do I find the ones I'm on?
The chooser puts the trackers you have ticked first, in their own group, open. Everything else is folded away by what it carries, with a count on each group, and the search box filters by name, language or software — type italian and you get Shareisland and ItaTorrents. Each tracker's search address sits behind its own Address button rather than in a box on every row.
Where do I add an internal group?
Click the group tag on any release and use the Add field in the menu — that is the quick way. The full list lives behind Internal groups… in the listing bar, next to Tracker rules…. Those are different panels: Tracker rules is naming rules and banned groups, Internal groups is which tracker a group belongs to.
Why is “Listed as internal at …” not a link for my tracker?
Because the script does not have that tracker's address, and it will not guess one — private tracker domains move, and a wrong link is worse than none. Add the tracker to your cross-check list and the link appears everywhere automatically, or put the address on its line: Tracker|group group group|https://address.
Do I have to set it up again on every tracker?
No. The trackers you are on, the trackers you have added and their rules, your internal-groups list, your comment templates, and which rules apply where all live in Tampermonkey's per-script store, which every site the script runs on can see — ordinary browser storage is kept per domain and cannot. Anything you had set up before this became shared is carried up the first time it is read, so nothing is lost. The audit, the decision log and private notes stay per tracker on purpose: those are a record of work on that tracker rather than a setting.
Can I capture one release on DarkPeers and compare it with one on LST?
Yes, as long as it is the same script running on both. The two vs slots live in Tampermonkey’s own per-script store, which is shared across every tracker the script runs on — ordinary browser storage is kept strictly per domain and cannot do that. Two different scripts, one on each tracker, means two separate sets of slots.
Two torrents have the same release name — how do I tell which is the real one?
Capture both. Where the names match, the panel stops comparing and concludes: a release is one file, so if the names match and the files do not, one of them is not that release, and it names what they differ in. What it will not tell you is which — neither page carries that. It also flags the honest caveat: a report pasted incompletely, or made by a different MediaInfo version, can differ in wording without the file differing.
What are the tags and encode settings for?
They are what the container says about how each file was made: the muxer that wrote it, the encoder and the settings it ran with, the encode and tag dates, the title tags. Two releases made the same way match; one that was remuxed or re-encoded afterwards usually names the tool that did it. None of it proves a file was altered — remuxing is normal, tags can be stripped, and plenty of tools write nothing at all — so it is shown as evidence to read, never as a verdict.
Will my internal-groups list go stale once I add something to it?
No — that used to happen and it was the wrong design. Adding one group used to save a copy of the whole directory beside it, which quietly pinned you to that day's list, so a group added to the shipped directory in a later version never reached you. What is kept now is what you changed — your additions, and anything you took out — layered over the directory the script ships with. New versions bring their groups with them; your own work stays on top. Clear list still puts the shipped directory back whole.
Two torrents both say 5.70 GiB. Are they the same file?
Not necessarily, and that is exactly why the comparison reads the byte count the page carries rather than the rounded figure it displays. A megabyte of difference disappears at two decimal places. Where both pages carry an exact count, the panel shows both and gives the gap in bytes; where a page carries none, none is worked back out of the rounded figure and presented as exact.
Do the shipped LUME and OnlyEncodes+ rules apply automatically?
No. They ship with the script but are not in use until you add them: Tracker rules… → Added trackers → Rule sets that ship with this script → Add. Until you do, luminarr.me and onlyencodes.cc are like any other tracker whose rules are not in hand — nothing is badged there.

It waits because adding a rule set changes which rules a badge cites, and a red ✕ quoting a rulebook that tracker never wrote is worse than no badge at all.

Once added, a shipped set is an ordinary added tracker — editable, exportable, removable — with one convenience: the profile carries its own tracker's address, so on that tracker it selects itself and you never touch the Rules dropdown. (If you have explicitly picked a different rule set for that site, your pick wins.)

So two trackers badge out of the box — DarkPeers and Zenith — and two more once you add them. On the other thirty-nine you choose a rule set, or add that tracker's rules yourself.

Is it 43 trackers or 60?
Both, counting different things. 43 is how many UNIT3D trackers the script runs on — the @match lines, where badges, the Inspector and vs appear. 60 is how many it can search for the cross-check, which adds the Gazelle-family and other non-UNIT3D sites — PassThePopcorn, HDBits, BroadcasTheNet, AvistaZ and the rest. It never runs on those; it builds a link you click. (43 trackers is 44 @match lines, because DarkPeers gets one with www. and one without.) Both numbers went down by two in 1.25.0: MoreThanTV and FearNoPeer closed, and a link to a site that is gone is a wasted click.
Where do those rules come from, and what is missing from them?
From each tracker's own published guide, and the script says which parts it does not have. Every one of LUME's own naming examples passes LUME's rules and the shared checks — that is a test in the suite, not a claim, and if a rule ever corrects one of the guide's examples the suite fails. OnlyEncodes' banned list keeps the reasons and dates its page gives; two groups that page marks as removed are deliberately not on it, because a list that keeps refusing a group the tracker has un-banned is worse than no list. No LUME banned list has been published to the script, so it carries none rather than inventing one.
Will it clash with my other userscripts?
It tries hard not to. If another script has already turned a group tag into a link, this one leaves that tag alone entirely. If the full DKOKTO Scene Edition is present, this one stands down.
Does a green ✓ mean the upload is fine?
No. It means the supported title checks passed. The media, the source history and tracker approval are not verified, and the tool says so rather than implying more than it knows.
Can it approve, reject or post for me?
No. Nothing is approved, rejected, posted, submitted or edited on the tracker. The decision log records what you decided, for you, in your browser; the reply builder writes a draft you copy yourself. One button types, and only into your own comment box — ⤵ puts your template in it, touches no submit button, and stops there. What goes up is what you press post on.
Where is my data kept?
In your own browser, each store with a fixed cap: notes, settings, the audit, the decision log, tracker profiles, the internal-groups list and your comment templates. Nothing is uploaded, and clearing your browser data clears it.

Reads, never writes

The whole point of the thing, and the part worth checking yourself in the source.

No requests

Nothing is fetched, crawled, uploaded or downloaded. Every link opens only when you click it. No API access, and no cookie, passkey or credential is read. It asks Tampermonkey for three permissions and they are all storage — GM_setValue, GM_getValue, GM_deleteValue — which is how the vs slots are shared between trackers. There is no GM_xmlhttpRequest and no @connect line, so it has no way to make a request at all.

No writing to the tracker

Nothing is posted, submitted, approved, rejected or edited on your behalf. It reads the page you are on and tells you what it found.

One button types, and only into your own comment box: ⤵ puts your template in it. It touches no submit button, adds to a draft rather than replacing it, and stops there — what goes up is what you press post on.

Local, bounded, yours

Every list it keeps lives on your machine with a fixed cap, and is never sent anywhere. Your setup — the trackers you are on, the rules you have added, your internal-groups list, your comment templates — is kept in Tampermonkey's own per-script store, so it is the same on every tracker. What you did on a particular tracker — the audit, the decision log, private notes — stays on that tracker. Both are storage on this machine, not a request.

Where the tool cannot know something it says so rather than guessing, and where it applies a rule it names the rule. That is the design, not a disclaimer.
