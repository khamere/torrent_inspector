What you get, in one minute

From a loaded listing to a reviewed torrent, with setup kept in one place.

1 · Start a review queue

A ✓, ✕ or ? beside each release name. Choose Review loaded titles to save the loaded links, or click a badge to open Findings. Use Next unchecked to continue.

2 · Find other releases

Find other releases opens one search pane: Title or Exact title, quality choices, an editable query and your saved tracker grid.

3 · Inspect the evidence

On a torrent page, what the name claims is compared with the report the uploader posted — resolution, codecs, audio, HDR, languages.

4 · Record your progress

Read Source check, add private notes and tick Reviewed by me. It records that you looked; it does not approve the torrent or hide naming errors.

One panel for the whole review

Drag the Review title bar to move the panel. Double-click it to centre again; reopening or resizing also recentres it.

TorrentLeech and FileList now use this panel too, without the old inline source list or automatic popup. Exact title searches keep the title, episode or year, and group once. For books, Exact title uses the compact title and year. ISBN remains available through Goodreads and Google Books. Use srrDB beside Compare releases, Goodreads and Google Books for books (ISBN when available), Discogs for music, and IGDB for games. AnimeZ joins CinemaZ, AvistaZ and PrivateHD in the tracker picker.

Language checks now recognize Primary Language es as Spanish, fixing a false Dual-Audio warning when Spanish and English audio are both present.

Source check in the tools bar opens this same panel. Its source tab shows the current filename, ID, folder and size above every comparison. Tracker rows start expanded with filename, ID evidence and return link, with separate counts for ID matches, name/size matches and mismatches. The last saved check on another page is clearly labelled below.

Individual torrent pages keep three actions: Inspect torrent, Find other releases, and Compare releases. Find other releases opens the new Find releases tab, with one editable query, Title / Exact title modes, quality choices and a grid of your enabled trackers. More lookup sites keeps the extra references close without filling the page with buttons.

Choose Review loaded titles on a listing, or Inspect torrent on a torrent page. The same panel opens in both places, even without a selected naming rule set.

Keep the evidence together

Switch between Findings, MediaInfo, Find releases, Source check and My notes. Notes save as typed; the report button stays in the footer. Open More tools for naming templates and the full Inspector.

Continue where you left off

Previous and Next unchecked follow actual links saved from the loaded listing and continue the panel on the selected page. The queue, current tab and notes stay with the torrent and tracker.

Your progress stays separate

Reviewed by me records that you looked. It never hides an error, changes a naming verdict or approves a torrent. Setup and the audit live under Settings & tools.

What it looks like

Taken from the project's own offline demo pages on the current build, with captions describing the same workflow. Made-up releases throughout; nothing from a real site. The same pages are live here, running the real script, if you would rather click than look.

Every loaded title, checked as the page renders

Listings, a member's uploads, bookmarks, playlists, the home page panels, a film's own page, and the moderation queues.

✓ The Money Pit 1986 1080p BluRay DTS 5.1 x264-iJiA

Passed. The title checks found nothing wrong — not a promise that the media, the source history or tracker approval were verified.

✕ Dead.Silence.2007.2160p.UHD.BluRay.DTS-HD.MA.5.1.DV.HDR.x265-B0MBARDiERS

Corrections found. The guide writes display titles with spaces, so the fix is written out for you — Dead Silence 2007 2160p UHD BluRay DTS-HD MA 5.1 DV HDR x265-B0MBARDiERS — keeping the dots that belong, like H.264 and DDP5.1.

? Bottle Top Bill 2005 540p WEB-DL AAC 2.0 H.264

Your judgement needed. A rule the title alone cannot settle, or a missing group tag. It asks instead of guessing; once you have looked and it conforms, Mark as conforming turns it green with a dotted ring that says the verdict is yours. Red is never marked over.

Whose rules?

Seven rule sets ship with it — DarkPeers, Zenith, LUME, OnlyEncodes+, HomieHelpDesk, MidnightScene and InfinityHD — each built from that tracker's own published guide and banned list, and each in force on its own tracker with no setup.

Anywhere else nothing is judged until you pick a rule set from the Rules dropdown, because a red ✕ quoting a rulebook that tracker never wrote is worse than no badge. The choice is remembered per tracker.

Add your own tracker

Tracker rules… takes a banned list pasted straight off the page plus tick-boxes for the common rules; profiles export and import as JSON, and Edit a copy lets you adjust a shipped one.

Build one on the web → No install needed: a tracker's staff can write, check and try its profile before handing it to anyone. That page runs the script's own modules.

Audit a whole sweep

Audit loaded titles, under Settings & tools, lists every name on the page with its faults as copyable text, and remembers what you checked while browsing so one audit can cover many pages.

Is this the same file the source tracker has?

Open a release on one tracker, then on another. The second page answers.

What it remembers

When you open a torrent page, the script remembers what makes that upload what it is: the report's Unique ID, the file name, the folder, the file list and the sizes. Kept for three days, in your browser.

Read all current comparisons

Open the same release on another tracker, then choose Source check. Current torrent details appear first; comparison rows start expanded with filenames, exact ID evidence and return links. Counts distinguish ID matches, name/size-only matches and ID mismatches. On individual torrent pages, the open panel updates as evidence loads and when you return from another tracker tab; no closing and reopening is needed. FileList details also uses the ID remembered from that torrent’s separate Media Info page, labelled as saved evidence. FileList and TorrentLeech refresh when you return to the tab. Last saved check on another page labels the older answer separately.

The Source check button

In the tools bar beside Nav and Inspect torrent. It opens the unified Source check tab with every current comparison in an expandable tracker row. The current fingerprint and last saved check from another page are labelled separately. Tick Only show this when I press Source check in the panel or notice to hide automatic notices.

More torrent pages, the same Review panel

TorrentLeech and FileList offer lookups and source evidence. CinemaZ, AvistaZ, PrivateHD and AnimeZ also support MediaInfo checks: Inspect torrent reads the loaded report, even when collapsed. The four-site adapter uses CinemaZ’s supplied markup; missing reports stay unknown. These pages have no invented naming rules.

It reads the page you went to. Nothing is fetched or opened for you; you follow the link, and the answer is read from that page's own text — including a MediaInfo tab you have not opened.

The MediaInfo already on the page, read properly

Alt+Shift+I in the standalone edition, or Inspect torrent in the tools bar. For pasted reports, open More tools → Open Inspector for advanced tools.

The title claimsChecked against the report
1080p, 2160pThe actual pixel dimensions, and progressive against the scan type
x264, x265, AV1The video format the report names
DD+ 5.1, DTS-HD MA, AtmosThe default audio track — DD and DD+ kept apart — and the channel layout against the reported count
DV, HDR10+Whether that HDR format is actually there
Dual-Audio, MULTiThe languages in the file, and subtitles for non-English audio
REMUXChapters, and a TrueHD track's compatibility track where the tracker requires one
S02, S02E03The episodes the file list actually holds: a gap in a season pack, a pack name over one episode, a file from another season

Compare two releases

Capture this release, open the other and capture that. The file lists and sizes compared to the byte, the two reports field by field, the tags and encode settings each file carries, and both releases in the block layout WinMerge or meld reads. It does not pick a winner: source and provenance are not in a report, and it says so.

Move around — Nav

Step by any number of listing pages or torrent IDs, or jump straight to one. Alt+Shift+N, or the button. Your filters and sorting stay on the address; only the page number changes. It follows a link, the same as typing the address.

[ MULTIPLE FILES ] — a pack in one click

A torrent with more than one file says so under its name; click the marker and the whole list goes to your clipboard with every exact size. One file gets [ SINGLE FILE · 2449415267 B ], the size to the byte.

The comment you type every day, once

A ⤵ button beside the comment box fills your template in, with {title}, {size}, {bytes}, {files}, {url}, {id}, {date} filled from the page. It writes into the box and stops — you press post.

Check a title before you upload

The naming box checks against the chosen tracker's rules, with reference fields that make the manual checks decidable and a searchable list of 288 streaming-service abbreviations.

Release groups, and where they are internal

Click the group tag at the end of any release name.

What the menu shows

Listed as internal at … — every tracker, not just one. Then that group's other releases on the tracker you are on, and the same search on each tracker you have chosen.

Where the list comes from

A community directory of internal groups ships with it — 854 groups across 152 trackers — dated and cited in the source. A directory can go stale, so every line reads “listed as internal at”, and a group that is not on it is reported as not on it rather than guessed at.

What you add sits on top of the directory, so a group added in a later version still reaches you.

Adding one

Click a tag with no home and the menu offers Add … to a tracker. The whole list is under Settings & tools → Internal groups… in the listing bar, as text you can paste a directory page into, one tracker per line: Tracker|group group|https://address.

A tracker's line is a link only when the script has its address; private tracker domains move, so it will not invent one.

If another script has already turned a group tag into a link, this one leaves it alone — nothing for two scripts to fight over.

Moderating a queue

On /torrents/pending, /torrents/rejected and /torrents/postponed.

A decision log

Each row takes Approved / Rejected / Asked the uploader / Left for someone else, with the date, so a sweep does not repeat itself. It records what you decided and changes nothing on the tracker.

A reply, written from the findings

Inside the badge dialog: the corrections numbered in the guide's own words, the release name and its link, a closing line. Manual checks appear as worth confirming, never as faults. Edit before you copy; nothing is sent.

What the page itself says

TMDB title and year against the name. The Type and Resolution filed against what the name claims. The file list — containers, samples, whether a season pack's files agree. Whether MediaInfo or BDInfo was posted. Dual-Audio against the page's own original language.

Where a rule is in hand it is cited and the finding is an error. Where none is, the finding says so. Nothing invents a rule to sound authoritative.

Installing it

  1. Install Tampermonkey if you do not have it.
  2. Click Install the script. Tampermonkey opens its own install page; read what it asks for, then press Install.
  3. Reload the tracker. The bar appears above the listing and the tools bar at the bottom right.

Which trackers it runs on

45 UNIT3D trackers, DarkPeers and Zenith among them, plus TorrentLeech, FileList, CinemaZ, AvistaZ, PrivateHD and AnimeZ for torrent-page review. Which sites run UNIT3D comes from HDVinnie's Private Trackers Spreadsheet; each address comes from the tracker itself or its Jackett or Prowlarr definition, and a site with no definition to read is left out rather than guessed at. Every tracker is a // @match line at the top of the file — delete one to stop it running there, copy one to add your own. The list is generated from the catalogue when the script is built, so the two cannot drift apart.

On the seven trackers with a shipped rule set the badges appear straight away. Anywhere else nothing is badged until you choose a rule set from the Rules picker or add that tracker under Tracker rules…; everything that needs no rulebook — the Inspector, Compare releases, the group tags, the cross-check, the source check — works there straight away.

Updates come to you: the script carries the address it is published at, so Tampermonkey checks on its own schedule and offers the new version. If you download the file instead, do not double-click it — Windows tries to run a .js file rather than install it. Keep one copy enabled; if the full DKOKTO Scene Edition is running on the same page, this one stands aside.

Tutorials

Things people actually do with it, start to finish. Every one is reading and clipboard only.

1 · Your first five minutes

From installed to badges on the page.

  1. Install and reload. On DarkPeers, Zenith, LUME, OnlyEncodes+, HomieHelpDesk, MidnightScene and InfinityHD the badges appear straight away. Anywhere else nothing is judged yet — that is deliberate.
  2. Choose whose rules apply here. The listing bar has a Rules dropdown with all seven sets. Pick the tracker you are on if it is there, otherwise the closest one. The choice is remembered per tracker.
  3. Tick the trackers you are on. Open Settings & tools → Choose trackers… and tick them once; the list follows you to every tracker the script runs on.
  4. Hover a badge. One line tells you why. Click it for the corrections and a copyable report.

2 · Is it the same file the source tracker has?

A release you are checking against where it was first published.

  1. Open the torrent page you are checking. The script remembers its Unique ID, file name, folder, files and sizes; choose Inspect torrent → Source check to see them immediately.
  2. Open the same release on the source tracker. Find releases searches by Title or Exact title; the link opens only when you click.
  3. Read Source check. Its expanded rows show the filename, ID and size evidence. A name/size-only match is labelled separately from an ID match. Automatic notices are optional.
  4. Go back. Back to that torrent returns you; the first page keeps that answer under Last saved check on another page, and a different Unique ID is a red finding there.
  5. Closed it too soon? Press Source check in the tools bar to open all comparisons in the unified panel. To hide automatic notices, tick the quiet checkbox in that tab.

A page that is a different encode of the same title is not about your upload and stays quiet on purpose; the Source check button says what the page was looked over for.

3 · Sweeping a pending queue

For moderators, on /torrents/pending.

  1. Read the bar first. It counts the errors, passes and reviews on the page.
  2. Choose Review loaded titles. The queue saves actual links from the loaded listing. Open a torrent from Review to continue with its own MediaInfo.
  3. Inspect the five tabs. Findings, MediaInfo, Find releases, Source check and My notes keep the work together. Copy report text stays in the footer.
  4. Tick Reviewed by me, then Next unchecked. Notes save as typed. This local progress mark never changes a verdict or approves a torrent; the separate decision log records moderation outcomes.
  5. Audit the sweep. Audit loaded titles under Settings & tools gives every name with its faults as copyable text, across the pages you have been through.

The amber ? rows are not failures — they are the checks a title alone cannot settle.

4 · Settling a trump with Compare releases

Two releases of the same film, and a decision to justify.

  1. Open the first torrent, press Compare releases under the release name, Capture this page as A.
  2. Open the other — on any tracker the script runs on — and capture it as B.
  3. Read the three tables: files and sizes with the gap in bytes, the two reports field by field, the tags and encode settings each container carries.
  4. Copy what you need. Copy the differences for a report; Copy both blocks for WinMerge or meld.

It will not pick a winner. Where both torrents carry the same release name and the files differ, it concludes one thing — at least one of them is not the release its name claims — and says plainly that neither page can tell you which.

5 · Teaching it a tracker's rules

When you are on a tracker it does not know yet.

  1. Try a shipped one first. Tracker rules… → Added trackers lists the seven under Built into the script; Edit a copy gives you one to adjust.
  2. Or build one from the form. The tracker's name and address, its banned list pasted straight off the page in whatever shape it publishes, and tick-boxes for the common rules.
  3. Check what you are about to add. Show the JSON prints it first; a pattern that will not compile is refused with the reason.
  4. Keep it or share it. Copy JSON and Save as file export a profile; the Paste or load tab takes one back.

A profile is data, not code: patterns are matched against a title, and nothing in one is ever run.

6 · Checking a title before you upload

Catch it in the box rather than in a postponed queue.

  1. Open the naming box — Inspect torrent → Findings → More tools → Open Inspector for advanced tools — and paste the title you intend to use.
  2. Pick the category and the tracker. The same title can be clean on one and rejected on another.
  3. Fill the reference fields — official title, original-language title, service — and several “cannot be decided from the title” reviews become answers.
  4. Paste your MediaInfo. The report is compared with what the title claims.

Already using Upload Assistant? Nothing here touches the upload form.

Questions people actually ask

Is it 45 trackers or 64?
Both, counting different things. 45 is the number of UNIT3D trackers supported. Six additional torrent-page adapters cover TorrentLeech, FileList, CinemaZ, AvistaZ, PrivateHD and AnimeZ. These offer Review without applying naming rules; the four AvistaZ-family sites also expose their loaded MediaInfo. 64 is the total number of searchable trackers, including sites where the script only builds a link you click. The userscript has 52 @match lines, with DarkPeers taking two.
Sixty-three trackers — how do I find the ones I'm on?
The chooser puts the trackers you have ticked first, open. The rest are folded by what they carry, and the search box filters by name, language or software — type italian and you get the Italian ones. Each tracker's search address sits behind its own Address button.
Why does my installed version differ from this page?
This page and its download are built together as 1.43.13. Installing the download updates the browser script; uploading the website files updates the public pages. Both must be deployed when publishing a release. An already-open demo tab needs a reload.
Do I have to set it up again on every tracker?
No. The trackers you are on, the rules you have added, your internal-groups list, your templates, the source-check setting and which rules apply where all live in Tampermonkey's per-script store, which every site the script runs on can see. The audit, the decision log and private notes stay per tracker on purpose: they are a record of work on that tracker, not a setting. Settings & tools → Backup… saves and restores the lot as one file.
Do the shipped rule sets apply automatically?
Yes, on their own trackers: each names its tracker's address, so on darkpeers.org, znth.cx, luminarr.me, onlyencodes.cc, homiehelpdesk.net, midnightscene.cc and infinityhd.net it selects itself. If you have picked a different rule set for that site, your pick wins. On the other thirty-eight you choose a rule set, or add that tracker's rules yourself; the group tags are marked everywhere regardless.
Where do those rules come from, and what is missing from them?
From each tracker's own published guide and banned list, and the script says which parts it does not have. Every worked example in a guide passes that guide's rules — that is a test in the suite, and if a rule ever corrects one of the guide's own examples the suite fails. A tracker's rule numbers are cited only where its page prints them; HomieHelpDesk's page numbers its sections, so its rules are cited by section. MidnightScene allows AV1, confirmed 24 September 2026; its older codec-list omission no longer raises a warning. No banned list is invented for a tracker that has not published one.
Two torrents both say 5.70 GiB. Are they the same file?
Not necessarily, which is why every comparison here reads the byte count the page carries rather than the rounded figure — the Compare releases tables, the file marker and the source-check banner all show it. Where a page carries none, none is worked back out of the rounded figure and presented as exact.
Two torrents have the same release name — which is the real one?
The source check answers that where the Unique IDs can be compared: the same ID is the same file, a different one is not. Compare releases shows what they differ in. What neither can tell you is which of two different files is the genuine release — no page carries that.
Why is “Listed as internal at …” not a link for my tracker?
The script does not have that tracker's address and will not guess one. Tick the tracker in your cross-check list and the link appears, or put the address on its line in Internal groups….
Will it clash with my other userscripts?
It tries hard not to. A group tag another script has already linked is left alone, and if the full DKOKTO Scene Edition is present, this one stands down.
Does a green ✓ mean the upload is fine?
No. It means the title checks passed. The media, the source history and tracker approval are not verified, and the tool says so rather than implying more than it knows.
Can it approve, reject or post for me?
No. Nothing is approved, rejected, posted, submitted or edited on the tracker. The decision log records what you decided; the reply builder writes a draft you copy; the ⤵ button fills your own comment box and stops there.
Where is my data kept?
In your own browser, each store with a fixed cap. Nothing is uploaded, and clearing your browser data clears it.

Reads, never writes

The whole point of the thing, and the part worth checking yourself in the source.

No requests

Nothing is fetched, crawled, uploaded or downloaded; every link opens only when you click it. No API access, and no cookie, passkey or credential is read. It asks Tampermonkey for three permissions and they are all storage — GM_setValue, GM_getValue, GM_deleteValue. There is no GM_xmlhttpRequest and no @connect line, so it has no way to make a request at all, and the build refuses to ship if one appears.

No writing to the tracker

Nothing is posted, submitted, approved, rejected or edited on your behalf. One button types, and only into your own comment box; what goes up is what you press post on.

Local, bounded, yours

Every list it keeps lives on your machine with a fixed cap and is never sent anywhere. Your setup is shared across the trackers it runs on; what you did on a particular tracker stays there. Both are storage on this machine, not a request.

Where the tool cannot know something it says so rather than guessing, and where it applies a rule it names the rule. That is the design, not a disclaimer.
